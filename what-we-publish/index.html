What we publish

Genre is a map, and we like to wander off it.

Here’s what we mean by the labels we use. If your book sits between them, send it anyway.

Literary fiction Fantasy Speculative fiction Horror Mythological retellings Dark academia Historical fiction Unclassifiable

Literary fiction.

Offbeat, daring, and willing to question the way things are. We want voice-forward, character-driven stories that interrogate the status quo through wit, satire, or an unusual form. Epistolary novels and other inventive structures are very welcome.

Books that capture the tone

  • Convenience Store Woman Sayaka Murata
  • Dear Committee Members Julie Schumacher
  • The Sellout Paul Beatty
  • A Visit from the Goon Squad Jennifer Egan
  • Yellowface R.F. Kuang

Fantasy.

Character first, worldbuilding second. We want to meet a person before we meet a map. If your opening pages are mostly exposition or lore, it won’t be for us. If a voice and a want pull us in, the world can unfold as we go.

Books that capture the tone

  • A Wizard of Earthsea Ursula K. Le Guin
  • Piranesi Susanna Clarke
  • Jonathan Strange & Mr Norrell Susanna Clarke
  • The Name of the Wind Patrick Rothfuss
  • The Poppy War R.F. Kuang

Speculative fiction.

Dystopian fiction, and stories that show the world through non-human eyes: animals, machines, and stranger things. We love speculative work that turns a mirror on our own world.

Books that capture the tone

  • The Handmaid’s Tale Margaret Atwood
  • Parable of the Sower Octavia E. Butler
  • Klara and the Sun Kazuo Ishiguro
  • Children of Time Adrian Tchaikovsky

Horror.

Horror that is genuinely creepy and leaves you unsettled. We like psychological dread that digs into the human condition, and we also like a real threat out there in the dark: an unseen presence, a haunted house, a gothic hush that gets under your skin.

Books that capture the tone

  • The Haunting of Hill House Shirley Jackson
  • Bird Box Josh Malerman
  • Hex Thomas Olde Heuvelt
  • Mexican Gothic Silvia Moreno-Garcia

Mythological retellings.

Retellings that pose a different question and hand the story to someone the original left in the margins, especially underrepresented characters. We like to see what changes when the myth is told from somewhere new.

Books that capture the tone

  • Circe Madeline Miller
  • I, Medusa Ayana Gray
  • Lavinia Ursula K. Le Guin
  • A Thousand Ships Natalie Haynes

Dark academia.

It’s our favorite genre, so here’s what we mean by it.

Dark academia is a closed world of devotion: a college, a department, a house of study, where a circle of brilliant people gives itself over to knowledge, beauty, and each other. The atmosphere is lush, with old stone, candlelight, libraries, and dead languages, and underneath it something is rotting. Ambition, obsession, and the need to belong carry characters across lines they can’t uncross, and the book is honest about what that costs.

We’re especially drawn to the character-driven and psychological: stories that run on who these people are and what they do to one another. We also love books that turn on the genre itself, asking who the ivory tower serves and what it takes.

Your story doesn’t need a campus. If it has the devotion, the beauty, and the rot, we want to read it. Dark academia, dark academia-adjacent, and dark academia with fantasy elements are all welcome.

Books that capture the tone

  • The Secret History Donna Tartt
  • If We Were Villains M.L. Rio
  • Ninth House Leigh Bardugo
  • Piranesi Susanna Clarke
  • The Historian Elizabeth Kostova
  • Babel R.F. Kuang
  • Katabasis R.F. Kuang

Historical fiction.

Historical fiction is welcome when it does any of the above: a fresh perspective, a voice left out of the record, or a story that questions how history gets told. We especially love it gritty.

Books that capture the tone

  • Babel R.F. Kuang
  • The Historian Elizabeth Kostova
  • The Meaning of Night Michael Cox

Unclassifiable.

Genre-bending, hybrid, and experimental work: books that cross boundaries and break the rules. Archival, surreal, whimsical, formally strange. If it’s a novel made of footnotes, letters, field guides, or something we haven’t named yet, we’d love to see it.

Books that capture the tone

  • House of Leaves Mark Z. Danielewski
  • Pale Fire Vladimir Nabokov
  • Invisible Cities Italo Calvino
  • Lincoln in the Bardo George Saunders
